## Projects

### Digna · *Your money's good here.*
**Founder & Product Owner · Oct 2026 – present · Live at [digna.dev](https://digna.dev)**

**The problem.** I was trying to pay for a cloud service to build my own product, and my prepaid card was declined. Then my debit card. The money was there, but the service didn't accept the kinds of cards I had, and nobody could tell me why. That happens every day to people who pay with debit cards, prepaid cards and money apps. Hotels, car rentals and online services turn them away, often with no reason given.

**What I built.** Digna gives you a **card for each bill**: the hotel, the rental car, the cloud bill. Each one pays from your own money, can never spend past the limit you set, and stops on its end date. No credit check and no debt. Before you pay, Digna tells you whether a place will take the card and why. If a place says no, it tells you exactly what to do next.

**What it does today** (with practice money, clearly labeled on every page):
- Make a card for each place, each with its own limit and end date. Pause it, raise it or close it in one tap.
- **Snap a bill**: upload a photo, PDF or Word file, and AI reads who it's from, the amount and the due date, then sets up a card for it.
- **Every decline explained.** Sample businesses follow real-world card rules, so you can see what a checkout would really say and why.
- **Ask Digna**: a money assistant on every screen. You can type or just speak. Ask it to "raise my hotel card to $180" and it sets that up, but **nothing changes until you tap Confirm.**
- Receipts for every payment, a calm private screen for declines, and a short celebration for approvals.

**How I made it trustworthy**
- **Money rules live on the server, not in the app.** Limits, end dates and balances are checked before any payment goes through.
- **Every person sees only their own data**, enforced inside the database itself.
- **Every form and request is checked** before it's trusted: amounts, dates, uploads and links.
- **AI costs are capped** per person and for the whole site each day, so spending stays predictable.
- **Honest by design:** practice money is labeled everywhere, invented sample businesses are labeled as invented, and the app never claims a bank or partnership that doesn't exist.

**How it was built.** The first version came from my AI app-building studio, where AI workers each built one part. I then directed AI coding tools through dozens of reviewed changes. I decided every feature, reviewed every change and approved it before it went live. I also measured and published who wrote what share of the code.

**Results**
- Launched a working, public product solo in **six days**, with a **3-minute film** now shared on social media.
- Shipped **four AI features**, all running on Meta's AI models: the assistant, bill reading from messy photos, voice input, and every image on the site.
- Built a **live user survey** whose results chart updates itself, showing counts only and never personal details.
- Wrote a **partner pitch** for card companies, hotels and fintech companies.

### AI App-Building Studio · *in stealth*
**Founder & Product Owner · Oct 2026 – present**

A platform where anyone can describe an app in plain words and get a real, working app back.

**How it works, at a high level**
1. **Talk it through.** You explain your idea to an AI mentor, which asks the questions a good product manager would.
2. **Plan and design.** A team of AI agents, each with one job, turns the idea into a plan and a design.
3. **Build and check.** Agents write the app, and separate agents safety-check the work.
4. **A person approves.** Nothing moves forward without a human sign-off at each key gate.
5. **See it live.** Each app gets its own private live preview in the cloud before it's shared.

**What I designed**
- **"The law":** a written rulebook every AI agent must follow, so the system behaves the same way every time and its decisions can be checked.
- **Human approval gates**, so people stay in control of what AI builds.
- A **credits and wallet system** for usage, a **secure vault** for each user's keys, and **private cloud previews**.
- A secure database where each person can only ever reach their own projects.

**Proof it works:** Digna's first version, about half of its code today, came out of this studio.

### Mirror Learning · *research*
**Creator · Nov 2026**

An original method for teaching new AI models **faster and more accurately**. The idea: plan the work first, split it across many small workers that check their own work, then audit everything on the way back up using information the workers never saw.

**How I ran it**
- **Fair races only:** both sides got the same model, the same information, the same computing budget and the same hidden final exam.
- **Every run written down**, wins and losses alike, with automated safeguard tests that must pass before any result counts.
- **One change at a time:** I refined the method through **nine versions**, each change backed by a recorded test, and dropped every idea that didn't hold up.

**Results**
- Beat standard training in **7 of 10** head-to-head races, a statistically significant result (p = 0.014, meaning about a 1-in-70 chance it was luck).
- Reached a best accuracy of **99.22%**.
- Against standard training with common expert add-ons, it **tied**. I published that too, along with what the results do and don't prove.

**How it evolved: 9 versions in 2 days**

| Version | What happened | What I decided |
|---|---|---|
| **1–4** | Wrote the method down over four drafts, defining every role and its one job | Plan first, one job per role, at most one redo |
| **5** | Realized the score could be quietly flattered by the same data that guided training | Added a separate final exam, used only once at the very end, so the reported score is honest |
| **6** | **Round 1: Mirror Learning lost.** I traced the loss to how the workers combined their results | Changed how workers share their progress, and added a check on every combined step |
| **7** | Ran small trials on three ideas before the next round | Kept all three. One new role stopped 10 of 12 false alarms and saved 8–14% of the time |
| **8** | Found that one of the self-checks was unfairly strict and was throwing away good work | Fixed it, and added a guide role that helps the model learn without handing it answers. It beat standard training in 6 of 8 races |
| **9** | Tested a rule for when to slow learning down, and re-tested every part | **Kept only what worked.** I dropped an idea that caught 82–84% of mislabeled examples, because it still cost accuracy overall |
| **Round 4** | Raced against a stronger standard method | **A tie.** Published as is |

## How I work with AI every day

- **I write the rules first.** Every project has a plain-language rulebook covering how it should look, how it should talk to people, what it must never do, and how decisions get made. Every AI tool follows it.
- **I direct, AI builds, I review.** I use Claude Code (an AI coding assistant) and my own studio's AI agents. Each change arrives as a proposal I review before it goes live.
- **I keep people in the loop.** In my products, AI can suggest and prepare, but a person confirms anything that matters.
- **I connect my tools.** My AI workspace links to GitHub, Supabase, Google Drive, Figma and Miro, and I run several AI sessions side by side, plus scheduled tasks for routine work.
- **I'm honest about results.** I publish what didn't work, label practice data as practice, and say who, or what, wrote the code.
